The Complete Season

Our 4-Step Annual Weed & Feed Roadmap

Minnesota turf demands precise timing. Here is how David & Amy keep your acreage green, dense, and protected against crabgrass all season long.

Application #1
Early Spring • May

Pre-Emergent Crabgrass Shield & Green-Up

Applied when soil temps hit 50°F before Memorial Day. Creates an invisible chemical barrier to halt crabgrass germination while waking up roots with high-grade spring nutrients.

Application #2
Early Summer • June

Broadleaf Weed Strike & Barrier Booster

Applied 4–6 weeks after Step 1. Knocks down active dandelions, clover, and creeping charlie, while extending your crabgrass defense into high-temperature summer months.

Application #3
Mid Summer • July / August

Heat-Stress Nutrition & Spot Control

Slow-release feeding formulated not to burn turf in midsummer heat. Spot treating breakthrough weeds while fortifying root crowns against summer drought and stress.

Application #4
Early Fall • September

Root Winterizer & Fall Weed Cleanse

The most important application of the year. Destroys stubborn fall perennial weeds before they store winter sugars, then feeds deep root systems for rapid next-spring green up.

Crabgrass Control

How Crabgrass Germinates & Why Timing Matters

Crabgrass is an annual grass that germinates, develops seed, and dies in a single season. In Central Minnesota, crabgrass begins germinating around Memorial Day as soil temperatures consistently reach 50°F.

Most pre-emergent products last 8–16 weeks. Heavy spring rains or early heat waves can break down barriers early, creating breakthrough near hot asphalt edges. We apply an early pre-emergent followed by a booster 4–6 weeks later to ensure complete season-long coverage.

Free Lawn Defense

The 2.5" – 3" Mowing Rule: Weed Control That Costs $0

Maintaining a mowing height of 2.5" to 3" is one of the most effective natural weed defenses you have. Taller grass blades shade the soil surface, blocking sunlight from reaching opportunistic weed seeds like crabgrass and dandelions.

Scalping your lawn invites weeds to take root. A thick, lush canopy combined with balanced fertilizer starves weeds naturally before chemicals are even needed.

Broadleaf Eradication

Tackling Creeping Charlie, Thistle & Dandelions

Broadleaf perennials like Creeping Charlie, Clover, and Thistle are notoriously stubborn. They are best eradicated with post-emergent treatments in early spring or early fall when weeds are actively transporting nutrients downward into their root crowns.

Once your broadleaf population is brought under control by our broadcast sprays, maintaining weed-free turf requires only minimal spot treatments each season.
